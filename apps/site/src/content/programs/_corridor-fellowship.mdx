The Corridors Fellowship Program helps UNLV and CSN students learn public transportation,
transit-oriented development, and civic advocacy by working on one real corridor at a time.

Each term, LVBT recruits a cohort of students, organizes them into small project teams, and pairs
each team with a peer lead and an outside professional mentor. The cohort works on a fixed ten-week
schedule and ends with a public demo night where teams share what they built.

Every cohort is tied to a specific corridor. Depending on the corridor and the team, fellows may
produce:

- a corridor data and analysis package
- station-area redesigns or site briefs
- short videos, photos, and stories from the corridor
- hearing trackers, testimony tools, or turnout materials

The point is not classroom practice. Fellows leave with a public portfolio piece, mentorship from
working professionals, and experience making work that residents, advocates, and decision-makers can
use.

Over time, the program builds LVBT's bench of trained advocates. Strong fellows can become peer
leads for later cohorts, help run future projects, testify at public meetings, or keep organizing
for better transit after the term ends.
