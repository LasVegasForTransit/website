## Overview

The Faith & Neighborhood Network builds relationships with congregations, neighborhood associations,
senior communities, and pedestrian-safety voices along transit-critical corridors. It starts from
shared local concerns rather than asking communities to adopt LVBT's agenda wholesale.

The first focus areas are Maryland Parkway, Charleston, senior communities across the Valley, and
groups already working on pedestrian safety.

## Motivation

Congregations, HOAs, and senior communities live with transit and traffic problems every day: kids
walking to services, seniors who no longer drive, dangerous crossings near a sanctuary, or a bus
stop that does not work for older riders.

Their civic voices carry a weight an advocacy organization cannot substitute for. LVBT has to build
trust before the next funding fight, so the people most affected are not being asked to show up only
at the last minute.

## Approach

LVBT will map each corridor, learn which communities are already active, and begin from the issues
those communities name: safer crossings, senior mobility, traffic deaths, access to services, or
transit along a corridor.

The work is relationship-based. LVBT will show up at community days, memorial walks, back-to-school
drives, and local meetings while bringing useful research, fact sheets, and visible support.

## Activities

LVBT will build a directory of corridor-based civic partners with notes on geography, community
concerns, meeting rhythms, and the issues each group has already named. The useful measure is not
the spreadsheet itself; it is whether partners return calls, share materials, host conversations,
and speak in their own voice.

People will see corridor briefings, meeting presentations, co-signed letters, neighborhood
statements, and faith or senior-community voices in the 2027 session record.
