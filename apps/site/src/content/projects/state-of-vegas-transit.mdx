## Overview

State of Vegas Transit will be LVBT's flagship annual research publication on transit and
transit-oriented development in the Las Vegas Valley.

Each edition will give residents, journalists, civic partners, public officials, and researchers a
shared reference on whether the region is becoming easier to live in without driving everywhere. The
report will connect bus service, street design, housing, jobs, zoning, heat, and public investment
into one readable account.

## Motivation

Transit and land use shape household costs, job access, school access, public health, climate
exposure, and neighborhood opportunity. Southern Nevada has the raw material for that story spread
across plans, budgets, public performance materials, development rules, capital programs, and public
meetings.

State of Vegas Transit will turn that scattered record into a public-facing annual account. It will
show where service is improving, where station-area policy is becoming real development, where
housing and jobs are growing near useful transit, and where public choices still reinforce car
dependence.

The report is built to travel. Journalists can cite it, residents can share it, partners can use it
in campaigns, and members can return to it when they want the larger picture.

## Approach

LVBT will build each edition from RTC plans and performance materials, city and county land-use
documents, TOD overlays, zoning changes, housing and jobs data, rider stories, field observations,
and photos from corridors people actually use.

The main report will stay comprehensive and document-style for people who need the full argument.
LVBT will also publish media-friendly versions, including a short executive brief, charts for social
media, corridor explainers, maps, a slide deck, newsletter excerpts, and issue-specific clips or
scripts for video.

The report will publish a methodology note, source list, and corrections contact. Official data,
LVBT field observations, rider stories, and LVBT analysis will be labeled clearly so readers can see
what each claim rests on.

## Activities

People will see a full annual report and a media package designed for reuse by residents,
journalists, partner organizations, and public officials. The package will include shareable charts,
short explainers, maps, slides, social posts, and a launch presentation.

The report will track whether more people can live near useful transit, whether station-area plans
are becoming mixed-use places, whether public investments help riders reach jobs and services, what
officials said would happen, what changed on the ground, and what LVBT wants residents to push for
next.
