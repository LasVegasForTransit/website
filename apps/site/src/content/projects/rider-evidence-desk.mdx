## Overview

The Rider Evidence Desk turns everyday transit experience into usable evidence. A missed transfer,
unsafe crossing, heat-exposed stop, confusing detour, or broken shelter becomes stronger when LVBT
can attach a date, location, photo, rider account, and follow-up record.

The project gives LVBT and its partners a stronger basis for public comment, press outreach,
corridor briefings, and direct conversations with agencies or elected officials.

## Motivation

Transit research is strongest when official performance data is paired with evidence from the
street. Rider reports and field observations show how service, stops, sidewalks, shade, information,
enforcement, and maintenance combine into a trip that works or fails.

A route can exist, a stop can be listed, and a project can be marked "in progress" while people are
still standing in heat, missing work, or giving up on trips. LVBT can make those conditions visible
in a way that public agencies, journalists, researchers, and civic partners can review.

Credibility is the point. The same story becomes more useful when it is tied to geography, timing,
documentation, and a clear request for response.

## Approach

The project will collect reports through a simple form, field notes from walk audits and tours,
photos with location context, and follow-up notes from agency conversations.

Entries will be tagged by route, stop, corridor, problem type, date, jurisdiction, and response
status. The record will help LVBT spot repeat problems, identify the right responsible body, and
make sharper asks.

Published notes will distinguish observed conditions, rider-reported experiences, and
agency-confirmed facts. Public pieces will include source notes, dates, limits of what LVBT knows,
and a way to send corrections.

## Activities

People will see issue briefs, photo-backed case notes, corridor summaries, and public follow-up logs
that show what residents reported and what response followed.

Strong entries will become public comments, agency meeting questions, neighborhood briefings, report
material, and press leads. The project succeeds when a problem moves from an isolated story to a
documented pattern with dates, locations, responsible bodies, and a specific fix LVBT is asking for.
