## Overview

Transit Accountability Briefs turn public promises into a usable civic record. When a funding
commitment, project deadline, board vote, or legislative promise affects riders, LVBT will preserve
the source, explain the stakes, and show the next moment residents can influence.

The briefs give residents, reporters, partner organizations, and elected officials a plain-language
trail from public record to public action.

## Motivation

Transit policy moves through agendas, budget votes, capital plans, committee hearings, staff
reports, and project updates. A single decision can move a major promise forward, weaken it, delay
it, or change who benefits.

Continuity is the point. A brief gives people the context they usually have to reconstruct on their
own: what was promised, what changed, who had authority, why timing matters, and how residents or
partners can respond.

## Approach

LVBT will track a focused set of promises and decisions where follow-through matters for riders,
funding, safety, access, or public trust.

The house style will require source links, publication dates, responsible bodies, plain-language
stakes, and a clear note on what remains unconfirmed. The work will draw from local meeting
coverage, legislative work, rider evidence, and annual reporting.

LVBT will keep the scope selective. A promise earns attention when it changes rider experience, the
funding picture, a major project, or the public's ability to hold decision-makers to their word.

## Activities

People will see short accountability briefs, a promise tracker for major commitments, and follow-up
notes when agencies or elected officials respond. Individual pieces may take the form of one-page
explainers, timeline notes, vote summaries, or follow-up memos depending on the decision.

The standard will stay consistent: a reader can identify the promise, source, responsible body,
current status, and next public opportunity without digging through a packet first.
