## About the role

You'll own LVBT's digital infrastructure — the website, integrations, automations, and the public-facing tools that help people understand what's at stake for Las Vegas transit. Some of it is maintaining and extending what exists. Some of it is building new things from a short brief and a lot of latitude.

The expectation is simple: you can take a loose idea — "we want members to be able to track their event attendance" — and come back with something that works, keeping us in the loop without needing hand-holding.

## What you'll do

The core of the job is keeping LVBT's website and digital tools working and getting better over time. Concretely, that means:

- **Maintaining lasvegasfortransit.org.** Astro, TypeScript, Tailwind, on Cloudflare Pages. You fix what breaks, catch what's slow or wrong before anyone else notices, and extend the site as the org's needs change.
- **Owning integrations and automations.** A lot of the work lives in the connections between systems — the website, the newsletter platform, the calendar, and whatever else we adopt. You'll keep those wired together and write the automations that save the team from doing things by hand.
- **Building public-facing tools.** Interactive visualizations, data displays, and member-facing features — a portal where members can track their event attendance, for instance — are on the roadmap. These are newer and less defined, so you'll have a real hand in deciding what they should be, not just how to build them.
- **Advising on technology decisions.** We're a small team, so when the org is weighing a tool or a direction, you're in that conversation and your judgment carries weight.

We work with agentic coding tools like Claude Code, and we'd want you to as well. If you're already building this way, you'll feel at home; if you're not yet, the willingness to learn matters more than the head start.

## What we're looking for

You don't need experience working with nonprofits. You do need:

- Comfort building for the web, and a willingness to work wherever the problem takes you rather than staying in one lane
- The ability to take a brief and figure it out — you ask good questions when you need to and run when you don't
- Familiarity with AI-assisted development tools, or genuine enthusiasm to build that fluency
- Curiosity about transit, cities, or civic tech — enough to care about what you're building
- Roughly 4 hours per week, with more when something is actively being built

## What you'll get

You'll have real ownership of infrastructure and tools that a real organization depends on. You'll work directly with the executive director and have genuine say over technical direction. The work is live, public-facing, and used by real people trying to change how this city moves.

This is a volunteer position.
