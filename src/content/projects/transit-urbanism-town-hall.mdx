## Overview

The Transit & Urbanism Town Hall puts candidates for state and local office on the record about transit, transit-oriented development, and the cost of living tied to both, before voters decide who represents them.

## Motivation

Voters rarely get a direct answer from a candidate on transit funding, zoning, or why rent and driving both keep getting more expensive. Without a forum built for it, those questions get buried under bigger campaign topics, and residents end up voting without knowing where anyone actually stands.

The 2027 session needs legislators who already committed to something in public. A town hall before the November 2026 election is where that commitment starts.

## Approach

LVBT will invite candidates from contested Assembly, Senate, and local races to a public forum built around three linked topics: transit service and funding, transit-oriented development and zoning, and the cost of living those two shape, gas, rent, and commute time. A moderator asks every candidate the same core questions so residents can compare answers directly.

## What people will see

A public event residents can attend or watch afterward, with a video or transcript posted so people who could not make it still see where each candidate stands before they vote.
