## Overview

The Transit & Urbanism Town Hall brings the Valley's transit and urbanism advocates and allies into the same room as candidates for state and local office. Candidates go on the record about transit, transit-oriented development, and the cost of living before voters decide who represents them.

## Motivation

Riders, urbanists, planners, students, and neighborhood groups are already doing this work across Southern Nevada, but there's no regular place where they actually show up in the same room together. Most of that organizing happens in separate meetings, separate coalitions, and separate social feeds that never intersect.

Voters also rarely get a direct answer from a candidate on transit funding, zoning, or why rent and driving both keep getting more expensive. Without a forum built for it, those questions get buried under bigger campaign topics, and residents end up voting without knowing where anyone actually stands.

The 2027 session will be stronger if legislators have already made public commitments. A town hall before the November 2026 election creates that record and gives the Valley's transit and urbanism advocates an actual room together.

## Approach

The event opens as a convening: tables and time for local transit and urbanism groups, riders, and neighborhood organizations to meet each other and compare notes before the candidate program begins.

LVBT will then invite candidates from contested Assembly, Senate, and local races to a moderated forum built around three linked topics: transit service and funding, transit-oriented development and zoning, and the cost of living shaped by gas, rent, and commute time. Every candidate answers the same core questions so residents can compare answers directly, and the format leaves real time for candidates to hear from advocates and residents in the room.

## Activities

The event will be open to the public, with a video or transcript posted afterward so people who couldn't attend can still see where each candidate stands before they vote. Beyond the forum itself, LVBT expects the convening to leave the Valley's transit and urbanism groups with actual connections to each other that outlast the event.
