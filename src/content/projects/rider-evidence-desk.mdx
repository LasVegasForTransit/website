## Overview

The Rider Evidence Desk complements official transit data with field evidence: missed transfers, unsafe walks to stops, heat exposure, unusable shelters, confusing detours, broken information, and the small failures that make a trip harder than it looks on paper.

It gives LVBT one organized place for rider reports, walk-audit notes, photos, agency responses, and repeat patterns that deserve careful public attention.

## Motivation

RTC already publishes official data, alerts, schedules, public records, and performance materials. LVBT's role is different: document rider experience and street-level conditions in a way that can be compared with those official sources.

The gap is different: official numbers do not always explain what riders experience before, during, and after the bus trip. A route can exist, a stop can be listed, and a project can be "in progress" while people are still standing in heat, missing work, or giving up on trips.

LVBT needs a disciplined way to turn lived experience into evidence that agencies, elected officials, journalists, and partner organizations can review and respond to.

## Approach

The project will collect reports through a simple form, field notes from walk audits and tours, photos with location context, and follow-up notes from agency conversations.

Entries will be tagged by route, stop, corridor, problem type, date, jurisdiction, and whether LVBT has asked anyone to respond. The point is not a giant data product; it is a practical evidence log that helps LVBT spot repeat problems and make sharper asks.

Published notes will distinguish observed conditions, rider-reported experiences, and agency-confirmed facts. Each public brief will include source notes, dates, limits of what LVBT knows, and a way to send corrections.

## What people will see

People will see issue briefs, photo-backed case notes, corridor summaries, and public follow-up logs that show what residents reported and what response, if any, followed.

The most useful entries will become public comments, agency meeting questions, neighborhood briefings, report material, and press leads. The project will be judged by whether it helps a real problem move from one report to a documented pattern with dates, locations, responsible bodies, and a specific fix LVBT is asking for.
