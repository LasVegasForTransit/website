## Overview

State of Vegas Transit is LVBT's annual report on transit and transit-oriented development in the Las Vegas Valley. It looks at buses, streets, housing, jobs, zoning, heat, and public investment together instead of treating transit as only an operations problem.

The report will offer a different perspective from agency plans and city reports. RTC and local governments explain their own programs; LVBT can explain what those programs mean for riders, neighborhoods, workers, students, and people trying to live without a car.

## Motivation

Southern Nevada already has official transportation plans, budget documents, dashboards, land-use reports, and project pages. Those sources matter, but residents also need an independent account of whether the Valley is actually becoming easier to live in without driving everywhere.

The missing piece is an accessible outside account: where transit service is improving or failing, where TOD policy is real or only aspirational, where housing and jobs are growing near useful transit, and where public decisions are still reinforcing car dependence.

LVBT needs a report that can be cited by journalists, shared by residents, used by partners, and revisited by members who want the larger picture without having to assemble it from every agency document themselves.

## Approach

LVBT will use official plans and public records as source material, not as the product. Core inputs will include RTC plans and performance materials, city and county land-use documents, TOD overlays, zoning changes, housing and jobs data, rider stories, field observations, and photos from corridors people actually use.

The main report will stay comprehensive and document-style for people who need the full argument. Alongside it, LVBT will publish media-friendly versions: a short executive brief, charts for social media, corridor explainers, maps, a slide deck, newsletter excerpts, and issue-specific clips or scripts for video.

The report will publish a methodology note, source list, and corrections contact. It will separate official data, LVBT field observations, rider stories, and LVBT analysis so readers can see what each claim rests on.

## What people will see

People will see a full annual report plus a media package that makes the same work easier to reuse: shareable charts, short explainers, maps, slides, social posts, and a launch presentation.

The report will answer questions agency documents do not always center: Can more people live near useful transit? Are station-area plans turning into actual mixed-use places? Are public investments helping riders reach jobs, school, health care, and groceries? What did officials say would happen, what changed on the ground, and what will LVBT ask residents to push for next?
