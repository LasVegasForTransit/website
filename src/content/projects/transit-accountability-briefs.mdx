## Overview

Transit Accountability Briefs track a smaller set of high-stakes promises and decisions: funding commitments, project deadlines, shelter and shade promises, safety fixes, legislative follow-through, and local votes that change what riders experience.

The project is not a replacement for official records. It is a civic translation layer for the decisions residents need to understand, question, and act on.

## Motivation

Official records can tell people what happened, but not always why it mattered or what to do next. A board agenda, budget vote, capital plan, or legislative hearing can move a major transit promise forward or quietly weaken it.

Residents need accountability that connects the dots: what was promised, who had authority, what deadline was missed, what money moved, and what public action would still matter.

## Approach

LVBT will choose a focused set of promises and decisions worth tracking. Each brief will cite the official source, explain the stake in plain language, name the responsible body, and identify the next public moment when residents can weigh in.

The work will draw from local meeting coverage, legislative work, rider evidence, and annual reporting. Each brief will include source links, publication dates, and a short note on what remains uncertain.

This project will stay selective. If a decision does not change what riders experience, the funding picture, or a major transit promise, it does not need a brief.

## What people will see

People will see short accountability briefs, a promise tracker for major commitments, and follow-up notes when agencies or elected officials respond.

Each brief will answer five questions: what was promised, what happened, what source shows it, who can change it, and what LVBT wants residents or partners to do next.
