We're trying to do something that still feels borderline impossible in this city: build a real public voice for transit in a region that has been built around cars for half a century. This page is the working theory behind that effort.

## What we want

We want a Las Vegas Valley where reliable, safe, and efficient public transportation helps people get to work, reach school, buy groceries, enjoy recreation, and build community without needing a car for every trip.

Light rail is part of the dream. The practical work starts sooner: protect and improve bus service, make major corridors easier to use, connect land use to transit, and build enough public demand that future rail becomes politically and financially possible.

## Why Las Vegas is different

Southern Nevada has no passenger rail and a relatively young civic memory around transit. Many residents have never seen high-quality transit as part of ordinary life, so education has to come before pressure.

The region is also approaching the physical and fiscal limits of outward growth. Better transit and more connected development patterns are practical requirements, not aesthetic preferences.

RTC has plans and professional capacity, but funding and political demand limit what it can deliver. LVBT can build the outside constituency public agencies cannot build for themselves.

## Where we focus

This is not a formal strategic plan. It is the public shape of the work while LVBT grows into one: what we pay attention to, who we try to reach, and how we keep the mission grounded.

Our focus is to:

- Make the case for better transit in plain language.
- Bring young people into the work early.
- Build the missing community for transit advocates and urbanists.
- Work with institutions without becoming one.
- Stay practical about the land use that makes transit useful.

## How we build power

We make transit legible. People need to know how the system works, what is at stake, who decides, and how ordinary residents can intervene.

We turn interest into membership. A large, organized base makes public demand visible to agencies, elected officials, reporters, and partners.

We show up consistently. Meetings, comments, partner briefings, and public testimony matter more when they are steady.

We connect transit to land use. A bus or rail line only works when the streets, housing, zoning, parking, and destinations around it support transit.

We fill gaps agencies cannot fill. Public staff have real constraints. LVBT can name the vision, build support, and create political cover.

## Values

Community and cooperation mean we prefer durable wins built with people over performative fights that leave the region harder to organize.

Authenticity means the work is strongest when volunteers, partners, and members can bring real talents and convictions to the table.

Courage means taking public stands for transit in one of the country's most car-dependent regions before success is guaranteed.

Empowerment means helping residents understand the systems around them and giving them practical ways to act on that understanding.

Sustainability means building processes, relationships, and public knowledge that keep working after a single campaign, meeting, or legislative session ends.

## What we are not

- We are not a transit agency. We organize public demand for better service, funding, streets, and land use.
- We are not anti-car. The point is choice.
- We are not partisan. Transit, safety, housing, affordability, and economic opportunity cut across party lines in Nevada.
- We are not here for one session. The 2027 Legislature matters, but the work continues after it.
